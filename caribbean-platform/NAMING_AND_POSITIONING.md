# Naming & Positioning — Working Brief

> Status: working draft for the founder's decision. This is the next
> exercise after the founding brief: naming and positioning the institution
> so the name sounds credible attached to a wealth ranking or award 20 years
> from now. Candidates below are starting points, not a decision; none has
> been cleared for trademark or domain availability.

## Positioning (settled by the competitor research)

**What we are:** the intelligence and media institution for Caribbean
wealth, business, families, influence and exceptional life — with luxury
coverage as one highly visible part of it.

**Public-facing idea:** the daily record of Caribbean success, power, taste
and achievement.

**What we are never:** a luxury Caribbean magazine (Caribbean Journal
already does a good version of that), a gossip site, a tacky display of
wealth, or a pay-to-rank plaque business.

**Voice:** authoritative, sophisticated, intelligent, discreet, global,
Caribbean and timeless.

## The naming test

The name must sound credible — in 2046, not just 2027 — in all of these
contexts at once:

1. A wealth ranking: *"the [X] Caribbean 100"* / *"[X] estimates the
   family's fortune at US$400–500M."*
2. An award plaque in a hotel lobby: *"Caribbean Hotel of the Year — [X],
   2031."*
3. A B2B data product a private bank pays for: *"[X] Intelligence."*
4. A citation by international media: *"according to [X]…"* (Bloomberg,
   FT, El País).
5. A black-tie ceremony: *"the [X] Awards Gala."*
6. All six product lines: [X] • [X] Wealth • [X] Intelligence • [X] Awards
   • [X] Live • [X] Society.

A name that works for a travel blog but fails on a bank's procurement memo —
or vice versa — fails the test.

## Criteria

1. **Institutional register.** The weight of Forbes, The Economist,
   Monocle. No "luxe", "VIP", "rich", "elite", "island vibes" vocabulary —
   those date fast and read as tacky, which the brand explicitly must never
   be.
2. **Pan-Caribbean neutrality.** Must not favor one island or bloc; the
   institution ranks Santo Domingo and Kingston and Nassau side by side.
3. **Cross-language grace.** Comfortable in English, Spanish and French
   contexts — the two largest initial markets (Dominican Republic, Puerto
   Rico) are Spanish-speaking. A name that a Dominican banker, a Jamaican
   hotelier and a Martinique developer all pronounce without hesitation.
4. **Sub-brandable.** Clean in front of 100 / 50 Best / Index / Wealth /
   Intelligence / Awards / Live / Society.
5. **Distinctive and ownable.** The "Caribbean + generic noun" namespace is
   crowded (Caribbean Journal, Caribbean Business, Caribbean National
   Weekly…) and weak for trademark purposes. A distinctive masthead is
   easier to protect and impossible to confuse.
6. **Timeless.** No trend words, no tech-startup phonetics, nothing that
   will feel dated on a plaque cast in bronze.

## Brand architecture: masthead-led (recommended)

Two viable models:

- **Masthead-led (Forbes model):** one institution name carries everything —
  Forbes 400, Forbes Travel Guide. Every franchise deposits equity in the
  single brand.
- **Franchise-led (World's 50 Best model):** franchises stand alone; the
  publisher stays invisible.

**Recommendation: masthead-led.** The 20-year objective is an *institution*
whose name itself confers prestige; that only compounds if every ranking,
award, gala and data product carries the masthead. Franchise names keep the
word "Caribbean" descriptively — the masthead confers, the franchise
describes:

- *The [X] Caribbean 100*
- *Caribbean Hotel of the Year — [X]*
- *[X] Intelligence*
- *The [X] Awards Gala*

## Candidate directions

### 1. The Antillean — lead candidate

The Antilles are the region's own classical name; "The Antillean" reads like
"The Atlantic" — literary, institutional, unmistakably regional without
naming any island. Natural across languages (Antillano / Antillais), and the
crowded "Caribbean + noun" shelf makes a distinctive masthead more valuable,
not less. Test lines: *The Antillean 100* • *Caribbean Hotel of the Year —
The Antillean* • *Antillean Intelligence* • *"according to The Antillean…"*

Coverage check: the name covers the initial markets almost perfectly.
Jamaica, Cuba, Hispaniola (Dominican Republic) and Puerto Rico *are* the
Greater Antilles; the eastern arc from the Virgin Islands down through
Barbados to Trinidad & Tobago is the Lesser Antilles. Of the eight launch
markets, only the Bahamas and Turks & Caicos sit technically outside the
Antilles proper (the Lucayan Archipelago).

Risks to check: acceptance of the name as a broad regional brand in the
Bahamas and Turks & Caicos — test with Bahamian readers; pronunciation for
first-time English readers; existing usage of "Antillean" in media names.

### 2. Tradewind / The Tradewinds

Double meaning — trade (business) + the winds that define the region;
timeless and dignified. Test lines: *The Tradewind 100* • *Tradewind
Intelligence*.

Risks: Tradewind Aviation is an established private-aviation brand serving
the Caribbean — a direct collision inside this exact audience; "tradewinds"
is heavily used across Caribbean travel and real-estate branding. Probably
fails criterion 5; kept as a foil.

### 3. The Caribbean Standard

Instantly clear, institutional register ("Standard" has newspaper lineage).

Risks: generic; joins the crowded "Caribbean + noun" shelf beside Caribbean
Journal and Caribbean Business, inviting confusion with the incumbents we
least want to be confused with; weak trademark. Strong fallback if a
distinctive masthead tests poorly.

### 4. Archipelago

Grand, geographic, dignified; *Archipelago 100* and *Archipelago
Intelligence* both sound plausible.

Risks: five syllables on a plaque; not exclusively Caribbean (any island
group); existing hospitality usage (e.g., Archipelago International hotel
group). Second-tier.

### 5. The Caribbean Register

"Register" is nearly perfect for the database DNA (Lloyd's Register, the
Social Register): a register is exactly what the wealth product is.

Risks: clunky on lifestyle coverage and award plaques; the "Caribbean +
noun" crowding again. Better held as a possible *product* name (the family
database) under another masthead than as the masthead itself.

### 6. Windward

Evocative, prestigious sound.

Risks: names only part of the region (the Windward Islands) — fails
pan-Caribbean neutrality for a Dominican or Bahamian reader. Cut unless
repositioned as a metaphor, which is a stretch for an authority brand.

## Recommendation

Shortlist **The Antillean** (lead), **The Caribbean Standard** (clarity
fallback) and **Archipelago** (distinctive fallback). Run all three through
the six-context naming test with a handful of people who match the audience
— a Dominican or Puerto Rican business figure, a Jamaican or Bahamian
hotelier, a private banker — then clear the survivor legally before any
public use.

## Diligence checklist (before deciding)

- [ ] Trademark search: US (covers PR), Dominican Republic, Jamaica,
      Bahamas, Cayman, Trinidad & Tobago, Barbados; plus EU/UK for diaspora
      reach — in media, data services and events classes.
- [ ] Domains and handles: .com plus regional defensive registrations;
      clean social handles.
- [ ] Conflict scan against existing Caribbean media, awards and data
      brands (Caribbean Journal, Caribbean Business, World Travel Awards
      properties, regional magazines).
- [ ] Language check: pronunciation and connotations in Spanish, French,
      Haitian Creole, Dutch (for future Aruba/Curaçao expansion).
- [ ] The six-context test above, on paper mock-ups: rich-list cover, award
      plaque, Intelligence product one-pager, gala invitation.
- [ ] Say it aloud in a sentence a banker would write: "According to [X],
      the family ranks fourth in the Caribbean." If it sounds odd, it fails.

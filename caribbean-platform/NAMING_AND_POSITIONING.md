# Naming & Positioning — Working Brief

> collisions and weaknesses are recorded below so they are not re-litigated.

> the core product: the brand can wait a little; the moat cannot.

## Positioning (settled)

**What we are:** the intelligence and media institution for Caribbean
wealth, business, families, influence and exceptional life — with luxury
coverage as one highly visible part of it.

**Public-facing idea:** the daily record of Caribbean success, power, taste
and achievement.

**What we are never:** a luxury Caribbean magazine (Caribbean Journal
already does a good version of that), a gossip site, a tacky display of
wealth, or a pay-to-rank plaque business.

**Voice:** authoritative, sophisticated, intelligent, discreet, global,
Caribbean and timeless.

## The reframe: the name does not need to be geographic

The first round searched for a clever word for the Caribbean. Wrong
assignment. The institution doesn't necessarily need a geographic name:

- Forbes is a surname.
- Bloomberg is a surname.
- Fortune is an idea.
- Monocle is an object/metaphor.
- The Economist describes a worldview.
- Robb Report inherited a name that became meaningful through the
  institution.

**Our name can acquire its Caribbean meaning rather than literally mean
Caribbean.** The sentence we are trying to own is:

> "According to [NAME]…"

If that works, almost everything else follows. The master ranking may not
even need "Caribbean" in the title: *The [NAME] 100 — the definitive
ranking of Caribbean wealth* is potentially stronger than any geographic
compound.

## First-round candidates — struck, with reasons

Recorded so the same ground isn't re-covered:

- **The Antillean — struck.** Elegant word, wrong asset to build on.
  Antillean.org already exists as a Caribbean publication whose business
  desk explicitly covers Caribbean business, finance, investment,
  technology, entrepreneurs and wealth strategies — far too close to this
  masthead, even if small or semi-dormant; a 20-year institution should not
  knowingly begin on pre-existing Caribbean media usage of essentially the
  same name. The geographic flaw compounds it: calling a Bahamian family
  one of the most important in "The Antillean 100" when the Bahamas is not
  part of the Antilles is the kind of small conceptual error critics repeat
  forever.
- **Archipelago — struck.** Worse B2B collisions: an established
  Archipelago enterprise-data company operates a platform handling millions
  of properties and trillions of dollars of insured property, and another
  long-established Archipelago company sells monitoring/data products.
  "Archipelago Intelligence" would walk straight into an already crowded
  data/enterprise namespace.
- **The Caribbean Standard — surviving but not loved.** Descriptive rather
  than ownable; substantial generic usage ("Caribbean Standard English");
  and the sentence test exposes it — "According to Caribbean Standard…"
  sounds like a newspaper, while "According to Forbes…" sounds like an
  authority. We are looking for the latter. Held only as a fallback of
  last resort.
- **Tradewind — struck in round one** (Tradewind Aviation collision inside
  this exact audience). **Windward — struck** (names only part of the
  region).

## Tightened criteria

The ideal name is:

1. **One word**, preferably 2–3 syllables.
2. **Distinctive enough to trademark** — no descriptive compounds.
3. **Pronounceable almost identically by an English and a Spanish
   speaker** (French a close third).
4. **No forced tropical imagery.** No palms, no paradise.
5. **No obvious luxury vocabulary.** No luxe, elite, VIP, prestige.
6. **Capable of becoming a proper noun whose meaning is created by us.**

And it must carry the full architecture without strain:

[NAME] • [NAME] 100 • [NAME] Wealth • [NAME] Intelligence • [NAME] Index •
[NAME] Awards • [NAME] Live • [NAME] Society

## The six-context test (unchanged, still the bar)

Credible in 2046, simultaneously, as:

1. A wealth ranking: *"The [NAME] 100"* / *"[NAME] estimates the family's
   fortune at US$600 million."*
2. An award plaque in a hotel lobby: *"Hotel of the Year — [NAME], 2031."*
3. A B2B data product a private bank pays for: *"[NAME] Intelligence."*
4. A citation by international media: *"according to [NAME]…"* (Bloomberg,
   FT, El País).
5. A black-tie evening: *"the [NAME] Summit & Honors."*
6. All product lines, including Society and Live.

## Brand architecture: masthead-led (settled)

One institution name carries everything, Forbes-style; franchises stay
descriptive underneath it. Island editions and the possible future
"[NAME] Homes" sub-masthead launch under the main masthead first — spun out
only once the masthead has authority to lend.

## The next naming round — assignment

Much more ambitious than round one:

1. **Generate 100–150 serious candidates** across several territories:
   - Ideas/qualities (the Fortune model): words for record, measure,
     permanence, standing, capital, light, navigation.
   - Objects/instruments (the Monocle model): instruments of measurement,
     navigation, record-keeping.
   - Latin/Romance roots legible in both English and Spanish.
   - Coined/constructed words with the right sound and no meaning to fight.
   - Surnames or surname-like forms (the Forbes/Bloomberg model), including
     invented ones.
   - Restrained maritime/navigational language that avoids tourist imagery.
2. **Eliminate ruthlessly:** existing media/data/finance usage (especially
   anything Caribbean or wealth-adjacent), trademark classes for media,
   data services and events, domain/handle reality, and linguistic problems
   in English, Spanish, French and Haitian Creole.
3. **Apply the sentence test and six-context test** to survivors.
4. **Present only the ~10 that genuinely pass the institutional test**,
   each with: the sentence test written out, collision notes, language
   notes, and the full product-line lockup.

Then trademark counsel on the 2–3 finalists before any public use.

## Diligence checklist (per finalist)

- [ ] Trademark search: US (covers PR), Dominican Republic, Jamaica,
      Bahamas, Cayman, Trinidad & Tobago, Barbados; plus EU/UK — in media,
      data services and events classes.
- [ ] Domains and handles: .com plus regional defensive registrations;
      clean social handles.
- [ ] Conflict scan against existing Caribbean media, awards and data
      brands, and against global data/intelligence vendors (the
      Archipelago lesson).
- [ ] Language check: pronunciation and connotations in Spanish, French,
      Haitian Creole, Dutch (for future Aruba/Curaçao expansion).
- [ ] The six-context test on paper mock-ups: rich-list cover, award
      plaque, Intelligence one-pager, Summit & Honors invitation.
- [ ] Say it aloud in a banker's sentence: "According to [NAME], the
      family ranks fourth in the Caribbean." If it sounds odd, it fails.

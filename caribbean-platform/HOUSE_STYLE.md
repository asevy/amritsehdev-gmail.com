# House Style — Narrative Authority

> **Status: working draft (2026-09-09).** The binding rule lives in
> [EDITORIAL_STANDARDS.md](EDITORIAL_STANDARDS.md) §14 (narrative never
> permits invention); this document is the craft guidance. To be refined
> against a reference publication when the founder shares it — the
> intended benchmark is the narrative style of the publication the founder
> mentioned (rendering power and family stories with atmosphere); once a
> link or sample article is provided, this guide gains a formal analysis
> of its headlines, openings, pacing, paragraph length, imagery, voice and
> endings.

## The voice: narrative authority

Not tabloid. Not breathless business journalism. Not academic. The company
underneath is technical — ownership graphs, filings, wealth models,
transactions, corporate records — but the reader should never feel they
are reading a database report. **The data is the machinery underneath.
The articles read as stories about power, families, fortunes and the
worlds they built.**

The Intelligence subscriber gets the graph. The reader gets the story.
Both come from exactly the same research.

## The transformation

Not:

> Rainieri Family Estimated at $X Million Following Punta Cana Expansion

But:

> **The Family That Built Punta Cana**

And an opening with atmosphere:

> Long before Punta Cana became shorthand for Caribbean luxury, the
> eastern edge of the Dominican Republic was a very different place. There
> were no sprawling resorts, no international airport delivering millions
> of visitors each year, and little reason for outsiders to imagine that
> this stretch of coastline would become one of the most valuable tourism
> corridors in the Americas.
>
> Then came the Rainieris.

Inside that beautifully written story sit the hard facts: ownership,
acreage, companies, partners, airport interests, transactions, family
succession, estimated wealth and sources.

## The arc

> cinematic opening → characters → history → money → ownership →
> conflict/challenge → transformation → present-day empire → next
> generation

Little moments make the world tangible: the hotel lobby where deals
happen, the old family photograph, the first parcel of land, the founder's
office, the boardroom, the marina at dawn, Santo Domingo society,
Kingston's old commercial families, Port of Spain's industrial dynasties.

**Never invent those details.** If we cannot source that the founder
actually sat in that office or made the deal over that dinner, we do not
manufacture the scene because it reads nicely. Every scene, quotation,
motivation and descriptive detail is reported or sourced (Standards §14).

## Headlines that earn the click honestly

The reader doesn't wake up wanting "Caribbean beneficial ownership
structures." They will absolutely read:

- The Five Families Behind Dominican Tourism
- How One Jamaican Family Turned a Pharmacy Into a $2 Billion Empire
- The Quiet Fortunes Behind Nassau
- Inside the Succession Battle Reshaping One of Trinidad's Great Business
  Dynasties
- Who Actually Owns Punta Cana?
- The Families Buying the Caribbean's Most Valuable Coastline

The headline promises a story; the story delivers the graph.

## Voice by section

**Luxury and hotels — never PR copy.** Not "This stunning new resort
offers 87 beautifully appointed suites…" but:

> **Why $600 Million Is Betting on This Corner of the Bahamas**

— then the family, the developer, the land, the architect, the financing,
the hotel flag, the villas, the marina, what rooms cost, what residences
are selling for, and whether this place actually matters.

**Restaurants** — culturally literate, not promotional. The reader should
trust the palate and learn the business.

**Society** — the reader finishes feeling they understand how the
Caribbean actually works; never gossip.

**Wealth and business** — the numbers carry the authority; the prose
carries the reader. Estimates appear with their ranges and confidence
labels even inside narrative features.

## The standard

The ideal article is enjoyable enough to read for pleasure and rigorous
enough to cite professionally.

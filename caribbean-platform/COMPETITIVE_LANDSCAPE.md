# Competitive Landscape

> First-pass desk research, September 2026. Figures and claims below come
> from that research; re-verify before citing externally (in a pitch deck,
> business plan or editorial copy).

**Bottom line: nobody is doing the whole concept.** There are competitors in
individual pieces of it, and one deserves particular attention — but there is
no credible Caribbean equivalent of Forbes' wealth infrastructure. That is
the whitespace, and it reshapes how we position and sequence the launch.

## The closest competitors

| Publication / organization | What it owns | What's missing vs. our concept |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caribbean Journal | Travel, hotels, restaurants, luxury tourism, awards | Wealth, families, ownership, business intelligence, influence database |
| Caribbean Business | Business/economy, especially Puerto Rico | Luxury/society + regional wealth rankings + families + serious awards ecosystem |
| World Travel Awards | Huge Caribbean hospitality awards | Journalism, people, wealth, influence, proprietary regional intelligence |
| World Golf Awards | Golf rankings/awards | Everything else |
| Forbes / Bloomberg | Individual Caribbean billionaires/business stories | No systematic Caribbean institution |
| Local society/business magazines | Island-specific personalities and lifestyle | Cross-Caribbean scale + data + credibility |

## Caribbean Journal — the most important competitor

Caribbean Journal is already a serious Caribbean travel publication:

- Its Caribbean Travel Awards are in their 12th year, with 41 categories in
  2026.
- It covers hotels, restaurants, destinations and tourism executives.
- Its reader-voted awards program generated more than 35,000 votes in 2024.
- It describes its audience as affluent travelers primarily from the U.S.,
  Canada and U.K.

**Implication:** do not launch as "the best hotels and restaurants in the
Caribbean." That territory is already occupied. (Note the structural
difference, though: its awards are reader-voted and its audience is inbound
travelers. Ours are editorially judged and inspected, and our core audience
includes the affluent Caribbean residents and families themselves.)

## Caribbean Business — worth watching

A real legacy brand: it has covered business, economics and politics for more
than four decades, particularly in Puerto Rico. It was recently relaunched
under new ownership and is explicitly trying to become a modern multimedia
business publication. Its current site remains overwhelmingly
Business / Technology / Economy / Finance, with special reports and a weekly
newsletter.

**Implication:** not our proposed product either — but the incumbent to
respect in Puerto Rico business coverage, and a reminder to move before
anyone extends a business brand region-wide.

## Awards: crowded but fragmented

There are already many Caribbean awards:

- World Travel Awards has dozens of Caribbean hospitality categories.
- World Golf Awards already awards Caribbean's Best Golf Course, Golf Hotel,
  Golf Real Estate Venue and individual island winners.
- Major properties like Casa de Campo collect awards from Forbes Travel
  Guide, Condé Nast Traveler, GOLF Magazine, USA Today, World Culinary
  Awards, Caribbean Journal and many others.

**Implication:** we should not try to differentiate merely by inventing
another Best Golf Course trophy. Differentiation comes from the combination
(the wealth/business/influence spine underneath the lifestyle coverage), from
editorial credibility (independent judging, anonymous inspection, transparent
scoring — never reader polls or pay-to-plaque), and from owning the calendar
as a system rather than one ceremony. See
[AWARDS_ECOSYSTEM.md](AWARDS_ECOSYSTEM.md).

## The whitespace: Forbes-grade wealth infrastructure

No credible Caribbean equivalent of Forbes' wealth infrastructure exists.
That is the really interesting opening.

Imagine opening a family page and seeing:

> **Rainieri Family — Dominican Republic**
>
> - Estimated Family Wealth: US$___–___M
> - Caribbean Wealth Rank: #___
> - Dominican Republic Rank: #___
> - Caribbean Influence Rank: #___
> - Primary interests: Tourism • Airports • Real Estate
> - Key holdings
> - Family tree
> - Next generation
> - Major transactions
> - Philanthropy
> - Board relationships
> - Historical wealth estimates
> - Latest news
> - Related companies

Then do that hundreds of times.

That is fundamentally different from a travel magazine — and it is the seed
of the core product: the family/company/wealth page, one per important
family, kept alive by daily reporting.

## The moat is the combination

What no one else offers in one authoritative institution:

| Pillar | The question it answers |
| --- | --- |
| **Wealth** | Who's rich? |
| **Ownership** | What do they own? |
| **Influence** | Which families actually matter? |
| **Business** | Who's winning, losing, buying and selling? |
| **Society** | Where does this community live, educate children, socialize and participate philanthropically? |
| **Achievement** | Which Caribbean people are succeeding globally? |
| **Luxury** | What is actually the best? |
| **Data** | How has all of the above changed over 5, 10, 20 years? |

## Strategic implications

1. **Don't lead with hotels. Lead with people, money and power.** Hotels,
   golf, restaurants, schools, art, property and travel make the publication
   beautiful and habit-forming and provide enormous advertising inventory —
   but the thing nobody can easily recreate after ten years is the database
   of Caribbean wealth, families, businesses, ownership, transactions,
   influence and relationships.

2. **Sharpen the positioning.** Not "a luxury Caribbean magazine" —
   Caribbean Journal already does a good version of that. Build **the
   intelligence and media institution for Caribbean wealth, business,
   families, influence and exceptional life**, with luxury coverage as one
   highly visible part of it.

3. **Plan the product family beyond the consumer publication:**
   [Brand] (publication) • [Brand] Wealth (rankings) • [Brand] Intelligence
   (professional database) • [Brand] Awards • [Brand] Live (conferences and
   summits) • [Brand] Society (invitation-only events). The Intelligence
   product could eventually be sold to private banks, investment firms,
   luxury developers, law firms, recruiters, family offices and other
   institutions that need to understand Caribbean capital — potentially much
   more valuable than advertising.

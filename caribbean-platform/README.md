# Caribbean Wealth, Business & Achievement Institution — Founding Documents

This directory holds the founding materials for **the intelligence and media
institution for Caribbean wealth, business, families, influence and
exceptional life — with luxury coverage as one highly visible part of it.**

Think Forbes + Bloomberg + Robb Report + Town & Country + World's 50 Best,
built specifically for the Caribbean. But not "a luxury Caribbean magazine" —
Caribbean Journal already does a good version of that. The company documents
who owns, builds, leads, influences and shapes the Caribbean, and the very
best the region has to offer. The objective is not short-term traffic: it is
authority, influence, proprietary data, prestigious intellectual property and
enterprise value that compound for decades.

## The moat

No one credibly combines all of this for the Caribbean today:

- **Wealth** — who's rich?
- **Ownership** — what do they own?
- **Influence** — which families actually matter?
- **Business** — who's winning, losing, buying and selling?
- **Society** — where does this community live, educate children, socialize
  and participate philanthropically?
- **Achievement** — which Caribbean people are succeeding globally?
- **Luxury** — what is actually the best?
- **Data** — how has all of the above changed over 5, 10, 20 years?

The thing nobody else can easily recreate after ten years is the database of
Caribbean wealth, families, businesses, ownership, transactions, influence
and relationships. Lead with people, money and power; hotels, golf,
restaurants, schools, art, property and travel make the publication beautiful
and habit-forming and provide the advertising inventory.

## North Star

Build the institution that journalists, investors, banks, family offices,
businesses and Caribbean leaders cite when they want to understand:

- Who has wealth.
- Who has influence.
- Who is rising.
- What is changing.
- Who is achieving globally.
- And what represents the very best of the Caribbean.

After 10–20 years of preserved credibility, the real asset is not website
traffic. It is that being ranked, profiled or awarded by the institution
carries reputational value across the Caribbean — and the proprietary wealth
database underneath it all.

The Intelligence product can eventually be sold to private banks, investment
firms, luxury developers, law firms, recruiters, family offices and other
institutions that need to understand Caribbean capital — potentially worth
more than advertising.

## Two inviolable rules (from day one)

1. **Nobody can ever buy an award, a ranking or a favourable review.**
   Advertising and sponsorship are structurally separated from editorial
   judgment. Sponsors may sponsor a ceremony or a category; they may never
   influence selection. This rule is what allows the awards to become worth
   something.

2. **Verified facts, estimates and editorial analysis are always clearly
   distinguished.** Every wealth estimate carries methodology and confidence
   levels. Rigorous sourcing throughout; no gossip, no sensationalism, no
   pay-to-rank. Private matters are covered only where legitimately public,
   and sensitive categories (political influence, healthcare, professional
   services) demand particularly careful, transparent methodologies.

## Initial markets

Dominican Republic, Puerto Rico, Jamaica, Bahamas, Cayman Islands,
Trinidad & Tobago, Barbados and Turks & Caicos — expanding selectively
across the wider Caribbean.

## Status and build sequence

**2026-09-08** — Founding brief, competitive thesis, content universe and
rankings/awards architecture locked at v1. Data model & methodology and
editorial standards drafted. First naming round struck; next round specced.

Conceptual planning is over. The build sequence from here:

1. **Data model & methodology** → validate with 10 real families across
   4–5 islands (the go/no-go test for the whole thesis)
2. **Name** → the 100–150-candidate round → brand/domain/trademark diligence
3. **Editorial standards** ratified
4. **Wealth methodology** finalized from validation-sprint lessons
5. **Database schema** implemented
6. **First 100 families** → **first 100 companies**
7. **Launch editorial calendar** → **website**

The first 100 family/company profiles matter more to the long-term moat
than the first 1,000 articles. Researching them tells us whether the
thesis works: whether sufficient public data exist, what can be
systematically collected, how long profiles take, where the gaps are, and
what stories naturally emerge from the database.
